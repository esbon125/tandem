\chapter{Known limitations}\label{cap:limitations}

\section{Speed}

The decoder runs at 0.5 to 0.8 times real time depending on the resolution.
The limit is in the core's motion compensation stage, not in memory or the
clock (measured in the project). It suits recorded clips, not live sources
that cannot accept flow control.

\section{Loaded quantiser matrices}

Streams that load a non-symmetric intra quantiser matrix in the sequence
header decode with visible errors from the first picture on. This affects 11
of the 39 conformance streams in the test bench, several of which load the
\emph{default} matrix. Symmetric matrices, and the usual case where the stream
loads none, decode correctly. It is a difference of the original core, under
investigation.

\section{Drift in predicted pictures}

In P and B pictures with a lot of motion the difference from the reference
decoder grows slightly along the GOP (mean absolute error up to $\sim$1 on
typical streams), with no appreciable visible effect. It too belongs to the
original core.

\section{Others}

\begin{itemize}
  \item No video output: the board has no video connector; the output is the
    network.
  \item MPEG-1 is not supported; one \emph{stuffing} conformance stream
    (\cmd{tcela-15}) produces no pictures.
  \item With TLS, delivery is limited to about 15 frames per second at
    704×480.
  \item Resolution changes within one clip: supported by the protocol, not
    exhaustively verified on hardware.
\end{itemize}
